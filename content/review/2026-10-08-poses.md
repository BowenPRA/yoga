# Pose library review, 2026-10-08

Second, independent pass over all 118 poses in `content/poses/**` (Ashtanga
primary series, Vinyasa, Yin). No ids, files, exports or cue/mod/safety
counts changed, so every clip id is where it was. The muscles of
`warrior-2`, `sleeping-swan` and `downward-dog` are untouched; the English of
`warrior-2` and `sleeping-swan` (the two poses with recorded audio and lesson
clips) is untouched.

Checks after the edits: `node scripts/check-content.mjs` 0 errors, 1 warning
(the existing anatomy-slug warning, unrelated to poses); `npm run lint` clean;
`npm run build` passes.

## What changed

About 150 field edits in 69 files.

- Nine `vi` names changed:

  | half-bound-lotus-standing | Nửa hoa sen đứng gập người | Đứng gập người nửa hoa sen | word order, and it now pairs with the seated one |

- English: `half-bound-lotus-standing` and `half-bound-lotus-forward-fold` had
  the same `en`. The standing one is now "Standing Half Bound Lotus".
- 20 places where one pose names another now use that pose's own `vi`. Examples:
  Rồng → Con rồng in the low-lunge and lizard safety lines; "Lỗ kim (số 4 nằm)"
  → Số 4 nằm in the swan and sleeping-swan mods; the English "Reclined Twist" and
  "Melting Heart" left inside Vietnamese lines → Vặn mình nằm ngửa and Trái tim
  tan chảy; Gập người đứng/ngồi in the Yin saNotes → the new forms; Cái cân in
  rooster → Hoa sen nâng người; Trồng chuối → Trồng chuối bằng đầu.
- The rule I applied: when the English uses a Yin name for the same shape
  (Eye of the Needle, Reclining Butterfly), the Vietnamese gives that Yin name
  as the Yin name ("Trong Yin, tư thế này gọi là Lỗ kim…"). When the English
  points to a different entry, the Vietnamese uses that entry's `vi`.

### Vietnamese register and naturalness (about 52)

No familiar forms of address were found: the checker passes and a scan found
none.
- Lines that read as translation now say what a teacher would say:
  - "ấn qua…", "đẩy ra sau qua gót chân", "kéo dài lên qua đỉnh đầu", "vươn qua
    đầu ngón tay" become "ấn … xuống sàn", "đẩy gót chân ra sau", "vươn đỉnh đầu
    lên cao", "dồn lực vào các khớp ngón tay";
  - "Bám rễ qua xương ngồi" becomes "Ấn hai xương ngồi xuống sàn";
  - "hướng hông vuông góc" becomes "hướng thẳng";
  - "hoạt động" for "active" becomes "chủ động";
  - "Đứng sau" for "follows" becomes "Nối tiếp sau";
  - "ưỡn sâu hơn trong" becomes "so với".
- Body words aligned with `content/anatomy`:
  - knee hyperextension "gối bị ưỡn ra sau" becomes "duỗi quá ra sau";
  - "gáy" becomes "phía sau cổ";
  - "bắp tay" for upper arms becomes "cánh tay trên";
  - "lòng bàn chân" becomes "gan bàn chân";
  - hamstring injury becomes "cơ gân kheo";
  - forehead becomes "trán", where "đầu" was used;
  - wherever a sentence has both a bolster and a knee, the knee is "đầu gối",
    never a bare "gối" next to "gối ôm".

### English (20)

The changes say "your" instead of "the" for the student's body in spoken lines.
Examples: "inside your knee", "top of your foot down", "just above your knee",
"Watch your back knee". There is also one grammar fix ("the backs of your legs
feel tight"). Good lines were left alone. Sleeping Swan keeps its "the knee"
lines because their audio and lesson clips already exist.

### Anatomy (7 muscles removed, none added)

| pose | removed | reason |
|---|---|---|
| lotus, bound-lotus, half-bound-lotus-standing | deep-rotators (working) | Lotus is held passively at end range, so the rotators are short but not clearly working. Kaminoff lists no working rotators for Padmāsana. |
| lotus-in-shoulderstand | triceps (working) | The arms only rest under the knees. |
| fish-pose | rhomboids (working) | Doubtful. The lift comes from the back extensors and the elbows. |
| extended-leg-pose | rectus-abdominis (lengthening) | It contradicts the breath line ("your belly … works hard"), and the muscle is both stretched and working here. |
| upward-plank | rectus-abdominis (lengthening) | The spine is close to neutral. Chest and biceps stay, because the shoulders are extended. |

The flagged claims I kept, with the reason:

- **Shoulder-pressing pose:** the adductors and rectus abdominis work. The cue itself is "squeeze your thighs into your arms".
- **Janu Sirsasana:** the adductors of the bent leg lengthen, because that hip is abducted.
- **Marichyasana:** the obliques work, in C and D.
- **Setu Bandhasana:** the quadriceps work to straighten the legs. The iliopsoas and rectus abdominis lengthen, as the body is in a full arch.
- **Tortoise:** the quadriceps work as the legs press against the arms.
- **Puppy:** teres major lengthens when the arm is overhead.
- **Handstand and headstand:** the trapezius works, because the shoulder blades lift and rotate upward.
- **Cow Face Pose:** the triceps of the top arm lengthen, as do piriformis and gluteus medius (hip flexed, adducted, turned out).
- **Seated twist:** piriformis lengthens for the same reason.
- **Malasana:** the erector spinae work to lift the chest.
- **Half moon:** the obliques work to turn the chest open, as in Triangle.
- **Crow:** the adductors work.
- **Deer:** the back hip turns in, which lengthens the deep rotators; the front hip lengthens piriformis.
- **Square:** deep rotators are still not listed. The hips are turned out there, which shortens them.
- **Snail:** the trapezius lengthens.
- **Reclined twist:** gluteus medius lengthens, because the top hip is adducted.
- **Shoelace:** the IT band is kept. Yoga Journal's Yin hip guide names it, and `content/anatomy` lists Shoelace under `it-band.stretches`.
- **Dragon:** the quadriceps of the back leg lengthen, because the hip is extended.
- **Revolved triangle:** gluteus medius lengthens.
- **Revolved side angle:** the iliopsoas and calves lengthen, since the back heel is down.
- **Uplifting pose:** the lats work to press the shoulders down.
- **Savasana:** only the diaphragm, which keeps the "no muscles" warning away.

These claims are kept, but no reference was opened for them. They come from
general anatomy, and Kaminoff and Long were not available online.

**Outside my files:** `content/anatomy/muscles-lower.js` lists `deep-rotators.works`
as ['warrior-2', 'lotus', 'bound-angle']. The lotus and bound-angle entries now
disagree with the pose files, and whoever owns anatomy should reconcile them.

## For Bowen: worth a native speaker's eye

1. **Chống đẩy thấp vs Tấm ván thấp for Chaturanga.** I switched to the commoner name. She may simply say "Chaturanga".
2. **Góc cố định / Góc cố định nằm vs Con bướm / Con bướm nằm.** Studios mostly say Con bướm for both Bound Angle and Butterfly. If she does too, swap the two `vi` and give the Yin one another name.
3. **The Ashtanga-only descriptive names**, which have no Vietnamese source: Ép vai, Gối ép tai, Bào thai and Hoa sen ngược và Bào thai (both use "embryo"), Cây cầu tựa đầu, Cái cày dang chân, Gập người thăng bằng, Thăng bằng nắm ngón chân cái, Gập người một chân co sau.
4. **Chiến binh khiêm nhường, Cánh cổng, Lật người ngả sau.** These are literal and may simply be said in English in Vietnam.
5. **"Thở vào lưng / ngực / hông" for "breathe into your back".** It appears about 40 times and is left as is. Is it natural, or would she say "hít thở vào vùng lưng"?
6. **"gối ôm" for bolster**, which is used everywhere. Some studios say "gối bolster".
7. **Smaller wording choices:** "tư thế đối" for counterpose, "dư âm" for the Yin rebound, "ngưỡng" for "edge", and "bấm các ngón chân" for "tuck your toes".
8. **"Gối" for knee.** Bare "gối" is kept for the knee in verb phrases (chùng gối, co gối). "đầu gối" is used wherever a bolster sits in the same sentence.
